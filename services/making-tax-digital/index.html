Making Tax Digital for Income Tax

We keep the digital records and send the updates. You do not have to buy the software or learn it.

Request a consultation

You can send the information in the simplest form you have: a photo, a PDF statement, or an email forwarded to us. You do not need accounting software.

This page is about Income Tax. VAT has its own rules, on the VAT returns page.

Who it applies to

Making Tax Digital for Income Tax applies to sole traders and landlords. Being a director does not, by itself, put you into it. It can apply if you are also self-employed or a landlord and your qualifying income is over the line.

Qualifying income is the gross income you get from self-employment and from property, added together, before expenses. Wages, dividends and most other income do not count towards the threshold.

HMRC looks at an earlier tax year, not a guess about the year ahead. The published timetable is:

  • Over £50,000 in the 2024 to 2025 tax year: Making Tax Digital for Income Tax applied from 6 April 2026.
  • Over £30,000 in the 2025 to 2026 tax year: it applies from 6 April 2027.
  • Over £20,000 in the 2026 to 2027 tax year: it applies from 6 April 2028.

If your income is under the line that applies to you, you stay on a normal self assessment return until it is not. Some people are exempt, including where HMRC accepts that it is not reasonable for them to use digital tools. Say so if you think that may be you.

Partnerships are not on this timetable yet. HMRC has said it will set that date later.

The source for the dates and the income test is HMRC: Find out if and when you need to use Making Tax Digital for Income Tax.

What we do for you

  • Keep the digital records of business and property income and expenses, from the photos, statements and emails you send
  • Prepare the quarterly updates and agree the figures with you before they are sent
  • Complete the tax return that is still due after the year end

Quarterly updates do not replace the tax return. The return is still due by 31 January after the end of the tax year. We do that with you as part of self assessment.

HMRC sets the quarterly dates. We put them in a timetable with you so an update is not a surprise.
